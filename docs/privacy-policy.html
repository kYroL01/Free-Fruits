Privacy Policy

Free Fruits is a foraging map for public fruit trees. A map like that only works if the trees are real, so photos carry GPS — but nothing about you leaves your phone.

The short version

  • No account. There is no sign-up, no login, no profile on any server.
  • No servers of ours. Free Fruits has no backend. Your trees, points and settings are written to your phone's storage and stay there.
  • No analytics, no ads, no trackers. No advertising identifier, no third-party analytics or attribution SDK, no crash reporter of our own.
  • Your live position is never recorded. It is read while the app is open, used on the spot, and discarded.
  • Uninstalling erases everything. Because the only copy is local, removing the app removes the data.

01Who is responsible

Free Fruits is an independent app published on Google Play under the package name com.freefruits.app. For anything in this policy, write to kraken86@proton.me.

02What the app stores, and where

Everything Free Fruits remembers is written to your device's own app storage, under the key free-fruits-store. It is not uploaded, backed up to us, or readable by us. That store holds:

  • Your display name, handle, avatar and invite code — all chosen in-app, none verified against anything.
  • Your points, tree count, strikes and redeemed rewards.
  • The trees you have logged, including their photos and the GPS coordinates attached to them.
  • Your pending upload queue and your alerts.
  • Your settings: units, dark mode, map precision, and the tracking toggles described in section 4.

We never see any of it. There is no endpoint for it to be sent to.

Free Fruits requests only the permissions it uses, and only at the moment it needs them.

04Location, in detail

Location is the most sensitive thing this app touches, so here is exactly how it behaves.

Your live position

Read only while the app is in the foreground, used to draw the map and measure the distance to a pin, then discarded. It is never written to storage and never transmitted.

Your trail

If the Keep my trail setting is on — it is on by default, and you can turn it off at any time under Profile → Privacy — the app keeps a short local history of where you have been, purely to compute distances and unlock nearby check-ins. It stays on the device. You can erase it at any time with Wipe trail, which deletes the trail and leaves your logged trees intact.

Precision of trees you log

Each tree you add carries the precision you choose for it:

  • Exact — the pin sits on the real coordinates.
  • Blur — the pin is offset, so the tree is findable but your exact spot is not.
  • Private — the tree is visible only to you.

The default is Exact. Change it before or after logging a tree.

Harvest counts

The Share harvest counts setting is off by default and stays off until you turn it on.

05What actually leaves your device

Two things, neither of which carries your app data.

Over-the-air updates

The app checks u.expo.dev for a new JavaScript bundle, a service run by Expo. That request tells Expo the usual things any HTTP request tells a server: your IP address, and which app version and platform asked. It carries none of your trees, photos, location or settings. Expo's handling of that request is covered by Expo's privacy policy.

Google Play

Distribution, installs and any crash reports you choose to send are handled by Google Play under Google's privacy policy, not by us. We do not receive your identity from Google Play.

On the shared map. In this version, trees you log stay on your own device — the map you see is served locally. If a shared community map is introduced, this policy will be updated and published before that release ships, and the precision setting in section 4 will govern what is shared.

07Your controls

All of these live under Profile → Privacy inside the app:

  • Set the map precision for your trees: exact, blurred, or private.
  • Turn the trail on or off.
  • Turn harvest-count sharing on or off.
  • Wipe your trail without losing your trees.
  • Export your data, so you hold a copy before you erase anything.

Android's own settings give you the rest: revoke any permission per-app, or clear the app's storage entirely.

08Your rights under the GDPR

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, the GDPR gives you rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection over your personal data.

Free Fruits is built so that these rights are exercised directly, without asking us: we hold no copy of your data, so there is nothing for us to retrieve, correct or erase on your behalf. The in-app controls in section 7 are how you access, export and delete. Where the app does process personal data on your device — chiefly location — it does so on the basis of your consent, given through the Android permission prompt and the in-app toggles, and withdrawable at any time by the same means.

You retain the right to lodge a complaint with your national supervisory authority.

09Children

Free Fruits is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them. Since the app collects nothing to a server, there is no such data for us to hold or delete — but if you believe a child has been harmed by the app's handling of data on their device, write to us and we will address it.

10Selling and sharing

We do not sell your personal data, share it with data brokers, or hand it to advertisers. There is no mechanism in the app by which we could: no analytics SDK, no advertising identifier, no server of ours that holds anything.

11Changes to this policy

If the app starts doing something this document does not describe — a shared map, an account system, a server of any kind — this policy will be updated and its effective date changed before that version reaches Google Play. Material changes will also be announced in the app.
